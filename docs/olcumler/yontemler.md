# Methods added on 21 Nov 2026: measurements

Numbers behind the claims made for the new modules (stress, buckling, GCMMA, SiMPL, Helmholtz filter, 0/1 check).
Raw records: [`yontemler.json`](yontemler.json). Every table is regenerated by
`python docs/olcumler/yontemler.py` (from the repository root, about two minutes).

**Environment.** Linux x86_64 (cloud container, 4 cores), Python 3.13.18, NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, torch 2.15.0
(CPU), SuperLU (no PARDISO, no gmsh, no GPU). This is **not** the reference environment of the bitwise baseline.
**Timings were taken while the test suite was running on the same machine**: they show orders of magnitude and
ratios between methods at best, and must be re-measured on an idle machine before they are quoted.

**Scope.** One problem per table, small 2-D grids, one start design. Nothing here is a benchmark study: the tables
show that the implementations behave as stated and how the methods differ *on these problems*. No statement about
which method is better in general is supported by them.

## 1. Optimizers and filters on the half MBB beam

90 × 30 elements, volume ≤ 0.5, SIMP p = 3 (E_min = 1e-9 E0), density filter r = 2.4 (cone unless stated), no
projection, 200 iterations, `tol` = 1e-3 (no run reached it: all stopped at the iteration limit). "binary obj":
compliance of the design thresholded at exactly 50 % volume, re-evaluated on the same mesh
(`optimization.benchmark.discrete_report`). "evals": state solves including those inside optimizer steps.

| Method | Grey compliance | Binary compliance | rel. to best | M_nd | Evaluations | KKT residual |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OC | 222.05 | 197.16 | 1.042 | 0.288 | 201 | 1.6e-2 |
| MMA (move 0.2) | 215.58 | 190.68 | 1.008 | 0.224 | 201 | 1.5e-3 |
| GCMMA (no move limit) | 215.62 | 190.58 | 1.007 | 0.223 | 411 | 4.9e-4 |
| GCMMA (move 0.2) | 215.60 | 190.61 | 1.008 | 0.223 | 407 | 1.5e-3 |
| SiMPL, Armijo | 216.70 | 190.54 | 1.007 | 0.234 | 420 | not available |
| SiMPL, Bregman | 216.45 | 190.45 | 1.007 | 0.232 | 513 | not available |
| SiMPL, no line search | 215.84 | 190.51 | 1.007 | 0.226 | 201 | not available |
| OC + Helmholtz filter | 219.13 | 189.17 | 1.000 | 0.277 | 201 | 6.8e-3 |

What the table shows, and what it does not:

- The binary compliance is 11–14 % **below** the grey one in every row. With p = 3 a grey element is charged its
  full volume but delivers x³ of the stiffness, so moving the same volume into solid elements is stiffer. The grey
  objective is therefore not the number to compare; the ranking by grey and by binary compliance differs (OC +
  Helmholtz is second-worst grey and best binary).
- MMA, GCMMA and the three SiMPL variants end within 0.1 % of each other in binary compliance. On this problem the
  conservative inner loop of GCMMA and the line searches of SiMPL cost about twice the solves and buy nothing
  measurable. Compliance with one volume constraint is the benign case for plain MMA; the reason to have GCMMA is
  the documented 2-cycle and strongly nonlinear constraints (§3), not this table.
- OC is 4 % behind after 200 iterations (KKT residual ten times larger): slower convergence, same family of design.
- The Helmholtz row differs from the others in the *filter*, so it is a different regularisation, not a better
  optimizer: its kernel is not the cone kernel (`design/pde_filter.py`: variance 11 % larger in 2-D, peaked centre).
- SiMPL reports no multipliers, so the study cannot compute its KKT residual; its convergence was judged by the
  objective only.

## 2. Linear buckling against the Euler column

Clamped–free column, width b = 4, length L = 160, thickness 1, E = 1000, ν = 0, unit compressive load spread over the
top edge (consistent nodal loads). Reference P_E = π² E I / (4 L²). Q4 elements.

| Elements across the width | DOF | P_cr / P_E | μ₁/μ₂ (exact 9) | μ₁/μ₃ (exact 25) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 486 | 1.1246 | 8.975 | 24.79 |
| 4 | 1 610 | 1.0308 | 8.970 | 24.76 |
| 8 | 5 778 | 1.0074 | 8.969 | 24.74 |
| 16 | 21 794 | 1.0015 | 8.969 | 24.74 |

The error falls by a factor of about four per refinement (second order), as expected for the bilinear element whose
bending stiffness is too high on coarse meshes. The mode ratios converge to 8.969 and 24.74 rather than 9 and 25;
the difference (0.3 % and 1 %) has the sign and size of shear deformation in the higher modes, which the Euler
formula ignores; this explanation was not verified against a Timoshenko solution. 3-D (4 × 4 × 64, side 2):
H8IM 0.9975, H8 1.0286 (`tests/toporlogy/test_buckling.py`).

Gradient of the KS buckling response against Richardson-extrapolated central differences: 8.5e-9 (2-D), 2.6e-9
(3-D), threshold 1e-6. With the dependence of the stress stiffness on the displacements cut, the gradient of λ₁ is
off by 96 % in 2-norm on the test problem: the term cannot be dropped.

## 3. Two stress-constraint strategies on the L-bracket

`examples/research/stress_lbracket2d.yaml` and `stress_al_lbracket2d.yaml`: 40 × 40 L-bracket (16 wide legs), load 1
on four nodes of the tip, σ_lim = 2.5, filter r = 2, projection β 1 → 8, the six-by-four elements at the load
excluded. Objective: volume. In the solid bracket the largest centroid von Mises stress is 0.49 σ_lim at the
re-entrant corner. (A first version of the example used σ_lim = 0.9, for which the solid bracket itself violates the
limit by 37 %: it was an infeasible problem, not a hard one, and was corrected.)

"max solid ratio": largest **unrelaxed** σ_vM/σ_lim over elements with x̄ > 0.5. "0/1": the design thresholded at
0.5 and re-analysed on the same mesh.

| | Aggregated (P-norm 8, adaptive scale, GCMMA) | Augmented Lagrangian (polynomial, MMA) |
|:---|---:|---:|
| Iterations / state solves | 120 / 743 | 600 / 601 |
| Volume, grey design | 0.195 | 0.181 |
| Constraint value the optimizer saw (relaxed maximum) | 0.999 | 1.001 |
| — its range over the last 20 iterations | 0.96 – 1.01 | 1.00 – 1.67 |
| Max solid ratio, grey design | 1.131 | 1.103 |
| M_nd | 0.229 | 0.182 |
| Volume, 0/1 design | 0.195 | 0.203 |
| Max ratio, 0/1 design | **1.715** | **1.025** |

Findings:

1. **Neither result is a stress-feasible structure.** Both satisfy the *relaxed* constraint on the *grey* design; the
   unrelaxed stress in the solid elements is 10–13 % over the limit, and after thresholding the aggregated design is
   71 % over. The relaxation x̄^q σ under-reports the stress of elements that are not fully solid, and 18–23 % of
   the design is still grey at β = 8. This is the density-to-geometry gap that the literature names as open
   (`docs/literatur_2026-11-21.md` §3); these two numbers measure it on one problem.
2. The augmented-Lagrangian design is lighter in grey, 4 % heavier after thresholding and within 2.5 % of the limit
   there. Its constraint value oscillated to the end (1.00–1.67): the run was stopped by the iteration limit, not by
   convergence.
3. The augmented Lagrangian **did not work** with the first settings. With the optimizer reset at every multiplier
   update (MMA asymptotes re-initialised every 5 iterations) the stress never settled in 600 iterations (largest
   relaxed ratio between 1.1 and 4.5 in the last 20 iterations, five parameter sets). Without resets, updating every
   iteration with penalty growth 1 %, it reaches the table above. `MultiplierUpdate(reset_optimizer=False)` is
   therefore the default. The settings are tuned on this one problem.
4. The monitor of the augmented-Lagrangian run uses the qp relaxation (x̄^0.5 σ) while its constraints use the
   polynomial form (x̄³ Λ(Λ² + 1)): the two disagree on grey elements, which is why a run could report its own
   constraints satisfied (max g ≤ tol) and a monitored ratio of 2. Compare strategies on the unrelaxed solid stress
   or on the 0/1 design, as above.
5. GCMMA used 6.2 solves per iteration on the aggregated problem (743 / 120).

## 4. Buckling constraint on a compression member

`examples/research/buckling_column2d.yaml`: 20 × 60, clamped base, load on the middle fifth of the top edge, volume
≤ 0.35, no projection.

| | Compliance | λ₁ | λ₂ | λ₃ | Iterations |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| compliance only | 10.63 | 0.0149 | 0.0939 | 0.2411 | 28 (converged) |
| with λ₁ ≥ 0.02 | 12.87 | 0.0218 | 0.0734 | 0.0749 | 80 (limit) |

The constraint raises the critical load factor by 46 % for 21 % more compliance. In the constrained design the
second and third load factors are 2 % apart: modes are clustering, the situation in which single eigenvalues stop
being differentiable and the aggregate over all computed modes is what keeps the gradient valid. Both designs are
grey (no projection, M_nd about 0.3); load factors of grey designs depend on the stress-stiffness interpolation and
were not checked on a 0/1 design.

## 5. What was not measured

- 3-D problems beyond the gradient checks and the Euler column; anything on the multigrid solver; PARDISO.
- Any comparison with reference codes (top88, topBuck250, PolyStress, the MFEM SiMPL example). The implementations
  are verified against closed forms, finite differences and their own stated properties, not against the original
  codes: the same limitation the README states for the core.
- Convergence to a tight tolerance: every optimisation above stopped at its iteration limit except one.
- Sensitivity to the start design, to the mesh and to the parameters (p, ρ, μ₀, α, β schedule).
